\begin{abstract}
We describe a reproducible, human-directed synthetic decision laboratory that evaluates constrained actions against an ensemble of opponent models using common-random-number Monte Carlo rollouts. It separates fixed observable utility and a matched baseline from policy-selection penalties for downside and empirical terminal entropy. Bayesian model weights use recorded simulated observations. This working paper defines falsifiable quantities and a conditional stochastic-stability proposition; it reports no real-world validation, novel convergence theorem for the implemented policy, or market prediction performance.
\end{abstract}
\section{Scope and system model}
Let $S_t=(v_t,r_t)\in[0,1]^2$ represent synthetic value and reserve. An administrator sets $a_t\in\{0,1/2,1\}$ and a model hypothesis $m\in\{0,1/2,1\}$. The illustrative simulator uses $S_{t+1}=\Pi_{[0,1]^2}(S_t+d(a_t,m)+\epsilon_t)$ with independent Gaussian perturbations and explicit clipping. Clipping changes the observation likelihood at boundaries. The hidden synthetic model is varied on a deterministic schedule, not a model of human behavior.
\section{Statistics and objectives}
Let $F=\{(v,r):v\ge .60,\ r\ge .20\}$. We estimate $\hat p_F(a,m)=N^{-1}\sum_i\mathbf1\{S_h^{(i)}\in F\}$ and empirical Shannon entropy of the $5\times5$ histogram of terminal states conditioned on $F$, when support is sufficient. No assumption implies that lower conditional entropy increases $\hat p_F$. Fixed reward is $R(S,a)=v+.35r-.04a$. The selection score is $\sum_m w_m[\widehat{\mathbb E}R_m-\alpha\,\widehat{D}_m-.1\beta\,\hat H_m]$, subject to a per-model empirical reserve-safety gate. Policy weights do not enter the observed reward used for evaluation.
\section{Belief update}
A model posterior is $w_{t+1}(m)=\bar w_t(m)\ell_m(o_t)/\sum_j\bar w_t(j)\ell_j(o_t)$, with switching prediction $\bar w_t(m)=.85w_t(m)+.15/K$ where $o_t$ is a recorded transition and $\ell_m$ the declared Gaussian approximation to its observation likelihood. This posterior is conditionally valid only if the assumed observation model is adequate.
\section{Conditional stability statement}
\begin{proposition}
Let $L:S\to[0,\infty)$ and suppose a policy and a transition kernel satisfy, for every admissible state, $\mathbb E[L(S_{t+1})\mid S_t]\le\rho L(S_t)+b$ with $0\le\rho<1$ and $b\ge0$. Then $\mathbb E[L(S_t)]\le \rho^tL(S_0)+b(1-\rho^t)/(1-\rho)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take total expectations in the assumed drift inequality and iterate the resulting linear recurrence.
\end{proof}
The proposition bounds expected $L$; it proves neither almost-sure convergence to a specific favorable basin nor that entropy reduction implies its premise. Establishing the premise for the implemented controller is future work.
\section{Budget-aware Monte Carlo allocation}
The fixed baseline samples every action-model pair $N$ times using common random shocks within each model. The optional Adaptive Computation Allocation (ACA) heuristic starts with a nonzero pilot for every model, evaluates all actions using the same shocks in a selected model, and allocates further batches to models with high posterior-weighted estimated uncertainty in the top-two action gap or proximity to the empirical safety threshold. It may stop before the fixed budget when a heuristic gap exceeds an uncertainty allowance and safety frequencies are away from the chosen empirical threshold. The gap criterion is \emph{not} a confidence sequence or a formal safe best-arm identification rule. In particular, finite-bin entropy estimation error, model misspecification and quantile estimation error are not rigorously covered.

A more principled target for future work is expected value of sample information divided by query cost:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathbb E[\min_a \mathbb E(L_a\mid D)]-
\mathbb E_{Y_q\mid D}[\min_a\mathbb E(L_a\mid D,Y_q)]}
{\mathrm{Cost}(q)}.
\end{equation}
ACA does not yet compute this nested expectation; it substitutes a transparent low-cost ranking proxy. Simulated action-rollout count and wall-clock time are distinct measures: reanalyzing partial samples can make an adaptive implementation slower even when it simulates fewer paths.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
Report matched-seed baseline uplift, constraint violations, posterior calibration, confidence intervals across independent seeds, simulator calls and CPU time. Compare against random feasible actions, action zero, an oracle synthetic-model policy and ablations of downside/entropy penalties. Real-domain experiments must be separately validated.
\section{Query-level allocation and a safety-information bottleneck}
A separate synthetic contextual best-arm laboratory (not the original additive multi-agent simulator) uses nine independent Bernoulli reward/safety query cells, three candidate actions and three hypothetical opponent regimes. Bayesian weights are updated from external synthetic evidence \emph{before} policy evaluation. The oracle computes its expected value analytically from declared model probabilities, and non-baseline actions are considered feasible only when their safety probability exceeds the declared threshold in \emph{every} modeled regime.

The conservative selection rule inverts a time-uniform, cellwise Bernoulli KL concentration bound with union control over all nine cells, both measured channels and all sample prefixes up to the prespecified cap. An unverified exploratory action is also reported solely as a research diagnostic; it is not an authorized choice. On the confidence event under the assumed iid model, any action certified safe by the lower-bound rule meets the stated model-conditional chance constraint. This does not imply correctness if the model class is incomplete or the underlying data are dependent.

\paragraph{A conditional information limit.}
In two worlds that differ only in one Bernoulli safety cell, with probabilities $p>\theta$ and $q<\theta$, a sequential procedure that certifies or rejects this cell with both types of error at most $\delta<1/2$ must satisfy, subject to the usual finite expected stopping-time and likelihood-integrability assumptions,
\[
 \mathbb E_p[N]D_{\mathrm{Bern}}(p\Vert q)\ge
 D_{\mathrm{Bern}}(1-\delta\Vert\delta).
\]
This follows because every observation outside that cell has zero KL divergence between the two worlds, the stopped transcript has expected KL $\mathbb E_p[N]D(p\Vert q)$, and processing that transcript into a binary judgment cannot increase divergence. This limited statement is about cell-level hypothesis discrimination, \emph{not} a lower bound on every algorithm that may choose a safe fallback without certifying the cell.

\paragraph{Measured negative baseline.}
In the prespecified 48-scenario toy set with cap 1024 samples per cell, full-grid fixed allocation used 9216 queries per scenario. At the equal 4608-query budget, uniform allocation selected the analytic feasible-action oracle in 26/48 cases, pure ACA in 17/48 and the half-uniform hybrid in 20/48. All conservative choices were analytically feasible in these sampled cases, but the allocator did not beat the uniform baseline in decision quality or wall time. These results are exploratory, domain-specific and insufficient for any general efficiency or external-validity claim; per-scenario JSON records and workflow provenance appear in docs/QUERY_LAB_RESULTS.md.

\section{Exact reward-only expected value of sample information}
To separate an information-theoretic decision metric from heuristic uncertainty scores, we also implement a conjugate model in which the unknown reward success probability for each independent model-action cell follows a $\mathrm{Beta}(1,1)$ prior. After $s$ rewards in $n$ samples, the posterior is $\mathrm{Beta}(1+s,1+n-s)$. The number of successes $K$ in a prospective batch of $b$ samples follows the corresponding Beta--Binomial predictive distribution. With the externally observed regime posterior held fixed, define $V^\star(D)=\max_a\sum_m w_m\mathbb E[p_{m,a}\mid D]$. For a specified reward query $(m,a)$, the exact one-batch sample information value within this reward-only Bayesian model is
\[
 \mathrm{EVSI}^{R}_{m,a}(b)=
 \sum_{k=0}^{b}P(K=k\mid D)V^\star(D,K=k)-V^\star(D).
\]
Because posterior means are martingales under the declared model and pointwise maximum is convex, Jensen's inequality yields $\mathrm{EVSI}^{R}_{m,a}(b)\ge0$. This is a claim about the \emph{reward-only} posterior optimal value; it does not imply an improvement in real realized reward, constraint satisfaction or sample efficiency. In an independent 24-scenario synthetic ablation at 720 queries per method, both uniform and exact reward-EVSI selected the analytically feasible-action oracle in 6/24 cases. The reward-EVSI implementation had higher measured mean wall time and more unsafe exploratory signals than uniform. This negative result is recorded with full raw outputs in docs/EVSI_REWARD_RESULTS.md. A narrow, one-batch joint reward/safety version of this calculation is addressed below; multi-batch globally optimal constrained information allocation remains unsolved.

\section{A constrained value-of-computation research module}
We define a joint reward-and-safety one-batch information calculation under \emph{independent} Beta--Bernoulli priors per hypothetical model--action cell. A simulation query of size $b$ returns separate reward and safety success counts $(K_R,K_S)$. Under the stated independent-channel model, their posterior predictive law is the product of the corresponding Beta--Binomial distributions. Let $\mathcal C(D)$ denote the set of actions meeting the original frequentist lower-confidence safety gate on data $D$, including the toy's known-safe no-op. Fix model weights obtained from external synthetic evidence. We evaluate the posterior reward attainable by a \emph{certified} action,
\[
 U(D)=\max_{a\in\mathcal C(D)}\sum_m w_m\,\mathbb E[R_{ma}\mid D].
\]
The one-batch signed constrained decision value of a query $q$ is
\[
 \Delta_{q,b}=
 \sum_{k_R,k_S}P(k_R,k_S\mid D)\,
 U(D,k_R,k_S)-U(D).
\]
Enumeration is exact within the declared conjugate independent-outcome model and the specified confidence-gated decision rule, up to floating-point errors. Unlike unconstrained reward EVSI, $\Delta_{q,b}$ need not be nonnegative: a safety observation can revoke a previously certified option. Our experimental priority uses $\max(0,\Delta_{q,b})/b$, with uniform coverage when every prospective one-batch gain is zero. The opt-in governor may stop after a minimum coverage when this myopic estimated gain is below an explicitly declared synthetic reward-unit per-query price. Such a stopping event is \emph{not} a proof that all multibatch information has zero value or that measured CPU time is optimized.

\paragraph{Constructive and negative evidence.}
A deterministic test demonstrates a toy state in which another reward batch cannot change the posterior-best reward action but another safety batch can make that action certifiable, giving positive constrained decision value. In the prespecified three-family, 36-scenario synthetic comparison at equal 1152 simulation queries, uniform and joint C-VoC each matched the feasible-action oracle in 14/36 instances; the C-VoC implementation used more measured wall time. The opt-in governor used 576 queries on average and matched the oracle in 10/36 instances. All certified choices were analytically feasible in this finite experiment. The detailed records and workflow provenance in docs/C_VOC_RESULTS.md limit these descriptive observations to the declared toy distributions.

\section{Finite multi-query certificate portfolios and the cost boundary}
One-batch information value can vanish when a non-baseline candidate needs safety evidence from several modeled regimes simultaneously. To address this delayed-evidence case, we construct a finite portfolio of future model--action query lengths $b_1,b_2,b_3$ for a candidate $a$. For safety-success counts $s_m$ among $n_m$ observations, let $K_m\sim\mathrm{BetaBinomial}(b_m,1+s_m,1+n_m-s_m)$ under an independent Beta--Bernoulli \emph{planning} belief. The probability that the fixed portfolio would complete the same time-uniform KL safety screening rule for all declared regimes is
\[
 P_{\mathrm{cert}}(a;\mathbf b,D)=\prod_{m=1}^{3}
 \sum_{k=0}^{b_m}P(K_m=k\mid D)
 \mathbf{1}\!\left\{\frac{s_m+k}{n_m+b_m}>\theta,\quad
 (n_m+b_m)D_{\rm Bern}\!\left(\frac{s_m+k}{n_m+b_m}\middle\|\theta\right)>
 \log\frac{4M N_{\max}}{\delta}\right\},
\]
where $M=9$ is the number of query cells. The product is valid only under the assumed independent safety-cell predictive model. The finite planner maximizes a \emph{heuristic} expected-positive-advantage-per-query surrogate,
\[
 Q(a,\mathbf b)=
 \frac{[\,\widehat V_a-\widehat V_{\mathrm{incumbent}}\,]_+
 P_{\mathrm{cert}}(a;\mathbf b,D)}{\sum_m b_m},
\]
over a coarse grid of affordable positive-length portfolios. This is not exact multi-stage sample information value: posterior reward is frozen within a plan, interim conditioning is not optimized, and the forecast may be misspecified even in the synthetic task. The final selected action is always independently screened on observed outcomes with the original frequentist safety rule.

\paragraph{Actual computation matters.}
Earlier query benchmarks pre-generated all potential random outcomes, even for unsampled cells. The current code generates only the actually queried Bernoulli reward/safety pairs from independent seeded per-cell and per-channel streams, preserving policy-comparable prefixes. An independent-seed 96-context synthetic benchmark with 576 actually sampled cells per policy recorded feasible-oracle agreement of 47/96 for the portfolio and 31/96 for uniform, with mean true constrained regret $0.07313$ and $0.10404$, respectively. Mean process CPU was approximately $0.01615$ s for the portfolio versus $0.00472$ s for uniform on that GitHub runner. A distinct calibration over 32 contexts selected the largest portfolio budget whose measured mean process CPU did not exceed uniform's CPU at 576 queries; this was only the mandatory 144-query pilot. On disjoint 48-context holdout, that CPU-matched portfolio matched the oracle in 4/48 cases versus uniform in 12/48. All of these are \emph{descriptive synthetic findings}, not general claims of safe or superior control. Raw workflow provenance and methodological limitations are retained in docs/MULTI_QUERY_RESULTS.md and docs/CPU_BUDGET_RESULTS.md. The evidence does not support replacing uniform as the current compute-efficient default.

\section{Limitations and research questions}
The toy model has three fixed opponent hypotheses, finite actions, boundary-dependent likelihood approximation, heuristic safety gate and empirical finite-bin entropy. Unknown strategies and unmodeled constraints can invalidate beliefs. Adaptive model-level computation allocation is implemented as an experimental heuristic, not a proven optimal value-of-information method; a globally optimal multi-batch constrained information scheduler, calibrated stopping certificates, MCTS for branching decision processes and a true multi-domain ontology remain proposed.
