\begin{abstract}
We describe a reproducible, human-directed synthetic decision laboratory that evaluates constrained actions against an ensemble of opponent models using common-random-number Monte Carlo rollouts. It separates fixed observable utility and a matched baseline from policy-selection penalties for downside and empirical terminal entropy. Bayesian model weights use recorded simulated observations. This working paper defines falsifiable quantities and a conditional stochastic-stability proposition; it reports no real-world validation, novel convergence theorem for the implemented policy, or market prediction performance.
\end{abstract}
\section{Scope and system model}
Let $S_t=(v_t,r_t)\in[0,1]^2$ represent synthetic value and reserve. An administrator sets $a_t\in\{0,1/2,1\}$ and a model hypothesis $m\in\{0,1/2,1\}$. The illustrative simulator uses $S_{t+1}=\Pi_{[0,1]^2}(S_t+d(a_t,m)+\epsilon_t)$ with independent Gaussian perturbations and explicit clipping. Clipping changes the observation likelihood at boundaries. The hidden synthetic model is varied on a deterministic schedule, not a model of human behavior.
\section{Statistics and objectives}
Let $F=\{(v,r):v\ge .60,\ r\ge .20\}$. We estimate $\hat p_F(a,m)=N^{-1}\sum_i\mathbf1\{S_h^{(i)}\in F\}$ and empirical Shannon entropy of the $5\times5$ histogram of terminal states conditioned on $F$, when support is sufficient. No assumption implies that lower conditional entropy increases $\hat p_F$. Fixed reward is $R(S,a)=v+.35r-.04a$. The selection score is $\sum_m w_m[\widehat{\mathbb E}R_m-\alpha\,\widehat{D}_m-.1\beta\,\hat H_m]$, subject to a per-model empirical reserve-safety gate. Policy weights do not enter the observed reward used for evaluation.
\section{Belief update}
A model posterior is $w_{t+1}(m)=\bar w_t(m)\ell_m(o_t)/\sum_j\bar w_t(j)\ell_j(o_t)$, with switching prediction $\bar w_t(m)=.85w_t(m)+.15/K$ where $o_t$ is a recorded transition and $\ell_m$ the declared Gaussian approximation to its observation likelihood. This posterior is conditionally valid only if the assumed observation model is adequate.
\section{Conditional stability statement}
\begin{proposition}
Let $L:S\to[0,\infty)$ and suppose a policy and a transition kernel satisfy, for every admissible state, $\mathbb E[L(S_{t+1})\mid S_t]\le\rho L(S_t)+b$ with $0\le\rho<1$ and $b\ge0$. Then $\mathbb E[L(S_t)]\le \rho^tL(S_0)+b(1-\rho^t)/(1-\rho)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take total expectations in the assumed drift inequality and iterate the resulting linear recurrence.
\end{proof}
The proposition bounds expected $L$; it proves neither almost-sure convergence to a specific favorable basin nor that entropy reduction implies its premise. Establishing the premise for the implemented controller is future work.
\section{Budget-aware Monte Carlo allocation}
The fixed baseline samples every action-model pair $N$ times using common random shocks within each model. The optional Adaptive Computation Allocation (ACA) heuristic starts with a nonzero pilot for every model, evaluates all actions using the same shocks in a selected model, and allocates further batches to models with high posterior-weighted estimated uncertainty in the top-two action gap or proximity to the empirical safety threshold. It may stop before the fixed budget when a heuristic gap exceeds an uncertainty allowance and safety frequencies are away from the chosen empirical threshold. The gap criterion is \emph{not} a confidence sequence or a formal safe best-arm identification rule. In particular, finite-bin entropy estimation error, model misspecification and quantile estimation error are not rigorously covered.

A more principled target for future work is expected value of sample information divided by query cost:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathbb E[\min_a \mathbb E(L_a\mid D)]-
\mathbb E_{Y_q\mid D}[\min_a\mathbb E(L_a\mid D,Y_q)]}
{\mathrm{Cost}(q)}.
\end{equation}
ACA does not yet compute this nested expectation; it substitutes a transparent low-cost ranking proxy. Simulated action-rollout count and wall-clock time are distinct measures: reanalyzing partial samples can make an adaptive implementation slower even when it simulates fewer paths.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
Report matched-seed baseline uplift, constraint violations, posterior calibration, confidence intervals across independent seeds, simulator calls and CPU time. Compare against random feasible actions, action zero, an oracle synthetic-model policy and ablations of downside/entropy penalties. Real-domain experiments must be separately validated.
\section{Limitations and research questions}
The toy model has three fixed opponent hypotheses, finite actions, boundary-dependent likelihood approximation, heuristic safety gate and empirical finite-bin entropy. Unknown strategies and unmodeled constraints can invalidate beliefs. Adaptive model-level computation allocation is implemented as an experimental heuristic, not a proven optimal value-of-information method; an exact expected-value-of-information scheduler, calibrated stopping certificates, MCTS for branching decision processes and a true multi-domain ontology remain proposed.
